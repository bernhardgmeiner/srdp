Text type · One of two tasks

The article

Write for real readers

LayoutDo’s and don’tsExampleKey tipPDFPractise
Word count
~250 or ~400 words
Register
Depends on the audience
Title
Required – catchy!
Structure
~5 paragraphs

Layout

Catchy title – rhetorical question, alliteration, bold statement
Introduction – ~50 words – grab the reader's attention immediately
Body paragraph 1 – ~50–80 words – content point 1
Body paragraph 2 – ~50–80 words – content point 2
Body paragraph 3 – ~50–80 words – content point 3
Conclusion – ~50 words – leave the reader thinking

Do’s and don’ts

Do

  • Know your audience (teenagers? general public? experts?)
  • Use rhetorical questions to engage readers
  • Address the reader directly ("you")
  • Talk about personal experiences and emotions
  • Use a topic sentence for each paragraph
  • Write a conclusion that leaves something to think about
  • Write an opening that makes people want to read on
  • Use exclamation marks and direct speech if the register allows it

Don’t

  • Don't be overly emotional or dramatic
  • Don't forget who you're writing for
  • Don't write a boring title
  • Don't write like a formal report (no "It is believed that…")
  • Don't ignore the publication context given in the task

Weaker

“In this article I am going to tell you about the advantages of doing sport.”

Stronger

“Ask anyone who has hit the wall at kilometre 30: running is at least half a mental game.”

Why it matters: An article has to pull the reader in. Announcing your plan sounds flat, while a concrete hook makes someone want to read on.

Key tip · Report vs. article: a report informs and recommends in a neutral tone. An article tries to make the reader feel something, so it can be personal and emotional. Always check where the article will appear: a school magazine needs a different voice from a serious newspaper.

Practise this text type

Pick a task, write it, then let the self-check look at your draft. The task bank has 4 article tasks.

Your school magazine is running a series on healthy technology habits and has asked for personal contributions.

Write an article (around 250 words) about a digital detox. Give your article a catchy title.

  • describe why you (or someone) decided to try it
  • explain what changed during the experience
  • tell readers what it taught you

An online magazine for young adults has invited contributions about working life.

Write an article (around 400 words) about how work is changing. Give your article a catchy title.

  • describe how remote work has changed daily life
  • discuss the benefits for employees
  • evaluate the challenges and what may be lost

A community website wants to encourage more young volunteers.

Write an article (around 250 words) about volunteering. Give your article a catchy title.

  • explain why volunteering is worth trying
  • describe a typical experience volunteers can expect
  • convince readers to take the first step
