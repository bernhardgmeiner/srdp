# Exam format and grading (SRDP B2, AHS & BHS)

_This source is part of the "Don't Panic, It's Just the Matura" B2 Writing Guide (matura.bernhardgmeiner.com). It follows the official Austrian SRDP documents for the written English Matura (AHS and BHS, level B2)._

How the written English Matura works and exactly how it is marked. This is the map for every other source in this notebook.

## The written exam at a glance

### AHS

You have 120 minutes for two tasks: one longer (~400 words, at B2 usually the essay) and one shorter (~250 words). Writing is one of four equally weighted sections of the written exam, alongside Reading, Listening and Language in Use.

- Total writing time: 120 min (total writing time)
- Tasks: 2 tasks (~400 + ~250 words)
- Time split that works: ~65 min for the 400-word task (incl. 5–10 min planning) · ~40 min for the 250-word task · the rest for proofreading both. Write your finishing times on the task sheet before you start.
- Word count tolerance: ±10%. That is 360–440 words for 400-word tasks and 225–275 for 250-word tasks. If you are further off than that, Task Achievement is reduced by one band.
- Dictionaries: At AHS, dictionaries and reference works – electronic ones included – are not allowed anywhere in the standardised exam. That is less scary than it sounds: the tasks are built so that B2 vocabulary is enough, and when a word will not come, paraphrase it – the grid rewards that.

### BHS

The BHS Writing section has three tasks, usually around 250 words each (each task states its own target, ±10%), and you get 195 minutes. Writing is one of three sections of the written exam, alongside Reading and Listening – there is no separate Language in Use section. One BHS perk: (electronic) dictionaries are allowed, but only during the Writing section. The text types are article, report, blog, e-mail and leaflet – no essay.

- Total writing time: 195 min (total writing time)
- Tasks: 3 tasks (~250 words each)
- Time split that works: three tasks in 195 minutes is roughly 55–60 minutes each, planning included. Read every prompt twice, note your finishing times on the task sheet, and keep about 10 minutes at the end to proofread all three.
- Word count tolerance: ±10%. Each task states its own target (usually around 250 words, so 225–275). If you are further off than that, Task Achievement is reduced by one band.
- Dictionaries: At BHS, (electronic) dictionaries are allowed – but only during the Writing section, not in Reading or Listening. Even so, the tasks are built so that B2 vocabulary is enough; when a word will not come, paraphrase it.

## The four assessment criteria

Writing is marked on four criteria, each on a 0–10 scale and each weighted equally.

**1. Task Achievement (Erfüllung der Aufgabenstellung)** — Did you do what the task asked?

All content points covered with supporting detail; text type conventions followed; appropriate title/greeting/closing; word count within ±10%.

**2. Coherence & Cohesion (Kohärenz und Kohäsion)** — Does your text hold together logically?

Clear overall structure; effective paragraphing; logical development; variety of linking devices; clear relationships between sentences.

**3. Lexical & Structural Range (Spektrum sprachlicher Mittel)** — How wide is your language?

Variety of vocabulary and structures; complex sentence forms; not repeating the same phrases; appropriate register; not lifting from the prompt.

**4. Lexical & Structural Accuracy (Sprachrichtigkeit)** — How correct is your language?

Grammar control; correct word choice; spelling; punctuation; accurate use of linking devices.

## The 0–10 band scale

**10 – Excellent:** Everything the task asks for, fully developed with well-chosen details. Conventions confidently used, wide range of vocabulary and complex structures, only occasional slips.

**8 – Good:** All content points developed with relevant details. Clear structure, good range and variety, errors are rare and never cause misunderstanding.

**6 – B2 minimum met:** This is the pass line of the official grid: all points developed but with limited supporting detail, a limited but appropriate set of linking devices, enough range including some complex forms, errors without damage to communication.

**4 – Below B2:** Content points addressed but not developed, mostly simple linear sentences, repetition and lifting from the prompt, or errors pile up beyond simple structures.

**2 – Far below B2:** Only parts of the task recognisable, very limited language, errors make parts of the text hard to understand.

**0 – Not assessable / veto:** No assessable performance – or the text fails to address the set task (see the VETO rule above).

## The word-count rule (±10%)

Every task states a target length. You have a tolerance of ±10%. For a ~250-word task that is 225–275 words; for a ~400-word task, 360–440. If you go outside the range, Task Achievement is reduced by one whole band before any language is judged.

## The veto rule

Task Achievement carries a veto. If a text fails to address the set task as a whole – it is off-topic, or an obviously pre-prepared text – Task Achievement is rated 0 and the other three criteria are not assessed at all. Writing the wrong text type on its own does NOT trigger the veto, but it costs heavily in Task Achievement. Simply failing to cover the content points does not by itself trigger the veto either; the trigger is missing the task as a whole.

## How an examiner actually reads your text

1. **First they picture the answer.** Before reading yours, the examiner sketches what a solid B2 response to *this* task needs: its purpose, its reader, the content points, the conventions of the text type. That sketch becomes the yardstick.
2. **They rate one criterion at a time.** Each of the four is judged on its own, often in its own reading pass. The same text can sit at 8 for Range and 6 for Accuracy – that is normal, not a contradiction.
3. **Task Achievement goes first.** It carries the veto: a text that ignores the set task scores 0 there and the other three are not marked at all.
4. **A band is a judgement, not an average.** Some descriptors count for more than others. Developing the content points matters more than the title. Nobody adds the descriptors up and divides.
5. **Length is part of Task Achievement.** More than 10% over or under the target, and Task Achievement drops one whole band, before a single language point is judged.

## B1, B2, C1 at a glance

The exam wants proof of B2. It helps to know what sits just below and just above.

**C1 (above the exam):** Fluent and precise well beyond what the Matura asks: complex ideas handled smoothly, idiomatic, register shifted at will, the important points made to stand out. Errors are rare and hard to spot.

**B2 (your target):** Clear and detailed across a range of topics: arguments developed and weighed up, a few complex structures, reasonably accurate. Occasional errors still slip through, but they never block the message.

**B1 (not quite there):** Short, simple and personal: familiar topics, ideas strung into a plain linear chain, reasons given only briefly, and noticeable mother-tongue influence throughout.

## "Appropriate" is not the same as "correct"

Two criteria can judge the very same word. Coherence & Cohesion asks whether a linking word *fits*. Accuracy asks whether it is *correct English*. A word can pass one and fail the other, and is then counted under both.

- "In contrary, online shops are often cheaper."
  - Cohesion (fits): As a signal of contrast it is placed exactly right, so it helps the flow.
  - Accuracy (form): But the phrase is wrong. Here English wants In contrast; keep On the contrary for directly contradicting a claim.
- "I cycle to school, beside it is faster."
  - Cohesion (fits): The link adds a second reason, which is what the sentence needs.
  - Accuracy (form): The correct linker is besides; beside means "next to".
- "Despite of the cost, the trip was worth it."
  - Cohesion (fits): The contrast marker sits in the right spot and joins the ideas cleanly.
  - Accuracy (form): It is either despite or in spite of, never despite of.

So reach for linking words freely (they lift Coherence), then check their *form* when you proofread (the same words are counted again under Accuracy).
