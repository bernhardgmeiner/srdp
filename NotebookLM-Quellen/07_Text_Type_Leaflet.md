# Text type: Leaflet / Broschüre (BHS only)

_This source is part of the "Don't Panic, It's Just the Matura" B2 Writing Guide (matura.bernhardgmeiner.com). It follows the official Austrian SRDP documents for the written English Matura (AHS and BHS, level B2)._

Inform and persuade at a glance. BHS · „Broschüre“ · informs & persuades.

## Quick facts

- **Word count:** ~250 words
- **Register:** Persuasive, reader-friendly
- **Title:** Required – plus subheadings
- **Key feature:** Headed sections + call to action

## How to lay it out

- **Main title** — names the offer or event clearly – and makes people want to read on
- **Short intro / hook** — ~30–40 words – say what this is and why it matters to the reader
- **Subheading + section 1** — one benefit or block of information under its own heading
- **Subheading + section 2** — the next point – short, scannable, one idea per section
- **Subheading + section 3 (optional)** — a further point if the word count allows
- **Call to action** — tell the reader the one thing to do next (come, sign up, get in touch)
- **Practical details** — date, time, place, price, website, phone – the facts people need

## Do

- Give it a clear title and break the text into short, headed sections
- Know exactly who the leaflet is for and write straight to them
- Sell the benefits, not just the facts – why should the reader care?
- Keep sections short and scannable – a leaflet is read standing up, not studied
- Address the reader directly ("you") and use imperatives: come along, find out more
- End with a clear call to action – the one thing you want the reader to do
- Add the practical details: date, time, place, price, how to get in touch
- Match the register to the audience, but keep it accessible – not slang, not bureaucratic

## Don't

- Don't write it as one long, unbroken block of text – a leaflet is broken into short, headed sections
- Don't drop the subheadings – they are what make it a leaflet at a glance
- Don't tell a personal story from your own life – a leaflet informs, it does not confess (that is a blog)
- Don't weigh arguments for and against – a leaflet promotes one thing, it does not debate it
- Don't slip into slang or txt-speak – persuasive is fine, sloppy is not
- Don't forget the call to action and the contact details – a leaflet with no next step is just decoration

## Key tip

A leaflet (called a „Broschüre“ on the official task sheet) is not one long block of prose. It informs AND persuades a target audience, and the layout does half the work: a clear title, short headed sections, direct address and a call to action. Picture the reader holding it at a bus stop – everything has to land in seconds. Keep the register persuasive but clean: warmer than a report, cleaner than a blog. You write the text only – a title, headed sections and a call to action, never drawings or layout.

## Useful phrases

**Attention-grabbing openers**

- Looking for something to do this summer?
- Ever wondered what goes on behind our doors?
- New to the area? Here is where to start.
- Something new is coming to your town – and everyone is welcome.
- Good news for anyone who loves…

**Describing & persuading**

- Whether you are a complete beginner or already experienced,…
- There really is something for everyone.
- It is easier – and cheaper – than you might think.
- Best of all, it is completely free.
- No experience needed – just come as you are.

**Direct address & calls to action**

- Come along and see for yourself.
- Sign up today – places are limited.
- Bring a friend and make a day of it.
- Find out more on our website.
- Do not miss out.

**Practical information & closings**

- When: Saturday, 14 June, from 10 a.m.
- Where: the main hall, Hauptstraße 1.
- Entry is free. / Tickets: €5.
- For more information, visit www.…
- Questions? Call us on… or drop by any weekday.

## Weaker vs. stronger

- Weaker: "This leaflet provides information about the annual town festival taking place in the market square."
- Stronger: "Live music, free food and the whole town in one square: the Seetal Festival is back, and you are invited."

_Why it matters:_ A leaflet has to grab a reader holding it for two seconds at a bus stop. The weak version describes itself like a report and gives no reason to care. The strong version leads with the benefits and speaks straight to the reader, which is exactly what a persuasive leaflet does.

## Annotated model text

**Model text: "Open Day at Schulzentrum Seetal – See Your Future in Action"**  
_~250 words · Persuasive register_

```
Open Day at Schulzentrum Seetal – See Your Future in Action
```

- "Open Day at Schulzentrum Seetal – See Your Future in Action" — Main title – names the event and sells it

> Not sure what to do after secondary school? Spend a morning with us and find out. On Saturday, 7 November, our whole school opens its doors: workshops, real projects and students who are happy to show you around. Come and see what studying here is actually like, with no pressure and no entrance test.

- "Not sure what to do after secondary school?" — Question aimed straight at the reader
- "workshops, real projects and students who are happy to show you around" — Sells the benefit, not just the facts

> Try Before You Choose
> Instead of reading about our courses, you can try them. In our workshops you can build a small circuit, plan a three-course menu or design a marketing campaign, all in a single morning. Teachers and senior students work alongside you, so there is no way to get it wrong.

- "Try Before You Choose" — Subheading – breaks the text into scannable sections
- "build a small circuit, plan a three-course menu or design a marketing campaign" — Concrete, appealing detail

> Meet the People, Not Just the Building
> Want to know what life here is really like? Ask the people who live it. Current students will show you the labs and workshops and answer your questions about lessons, homework and life after graduation.

- "Meet the People, Not Just the Building" — Second subheading – parallel and benefit-led
- "Ask the people who live it." — Direct address keeps the reader involved

> Something for Every Interest
> Whether you are drawn to technology, business or hospitality, there is a taster session waiting for you. Try your hand at coding, run a mini pop-up shop, or cook a small dish in our training kitchen. You will leave knowing which path actually suits you, not just which one sounds good in a brochure.

- "Whether you are drawn to technology, business or hospitality" — Speaks to HTL, HAK and HLW readers alike
- "Try your hand at coding, run a mini pop-up shop, or cook a small dish" — Concrete, varied activities keep it persuasive

> Come and See for Yourself
> Bring your parents, bring a friend, bring your questions. Entry is free and no registration is needed.
> 
> When: Saturday, 7 November, 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.
> Where: Schulzentrum Seetal, Bildungsweg 1, 1234 Seetal
> More information: www.schulzentrum-seetal.example

- "Come and See for Yourself" — Call to action – tells the reader exactly what to do
- "Bring your parents, bring a friend, bring your questions." — Imperatives + repetition – persuasive leaflet style
- "When: Saturday, 7 November, 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.
Where: Schulzentrum Seetal, Bildungsweg 1, 1234 Seetal
More information: www.schulzentrum-seetal.example" — Practical details – the facts people need

### The "honest model": a realistic pass, not a perfect one

**Information about the Charity Run**  
_~170 words · a realistic pass, not a model of perfection_

Same idea as the models above, but for the leaflet. This one would scrape a pass, yet it leaks marks exactly where BHS students lose them: a flat, report-style title, no subheadings, and no real call to action. The markers show where.

> Information about the Charity Run[1]

> Our school organises a charity run and we want that many people are coming.[2] In this leaflet I give you some informations[3] about the event.

> The run is since three years[4] a tradition at our school. Last year we collected over 2,000 Euro for a local animal shelter, and this year we want to collect even more. If you take part, you help animals who has[5] no home. You can run 5 or 10 kilometres, it depends on you.[6] There is also music and a buffet with cakes and drinks.

> The atmosphere is always great and it makes a lot of fun.[7] Your family and friends can come and watch too. So I think everybody should come, because it is for a good thing and also very funny.[8]

> It would be nice if many people come.[9]

**What each marked spot [n] costs, and the stronger version:**

1. A leaflet needs a title that pulls the reader in and short sections with their own subheadings. This one has a flat, notice-style title and runs as a single block with no subheadings at all – the biggest leaflet convention to lose, and it costs Task Achievement.
2. 'we want that many people are coming' is German word order ('wir wollen, dass...'). English: 'we want many people to come'. And 'In this leaflet I give you information' is flat – a leaflet opens with a hook, not a table of contents.
3. 'informations' does not exist – 'information' is uncountable.
4. 'is since three years' – the classic since/for trap, plus tense: 'has been a tradition for three years'.
5. 'animals who has no home' – animals take 'that', and it needs plural agreement: 'animals that have no home'.
6. 'You can run 5 or 10 kilometres, it depends on you.' is a comma splice (two complete sentences joined by only a comma). Use a full stop, and turn it into a benefit rather than a flat statement.
7. 'it makes a lot of fun' is a direct translation of 'macht Spaß'. English: 'it is a lot of fun' or 'great fun'.
8. 'very funny' is a false friend: funny = lustig (comical). Here you mean 'fun' (enjoyable).
9. This is where the leaflet really leaks: there is no call to action and no practical details. A leaflet must tell the reader exactly what to do and give the facts – When? Where? How do I sign up? Ending on 'it would be nice if many people come' leaves the reader with nothing to act on.

**Where would this land?** Where would this land? Task Achievement around 4–5 – the content is there, but the missing subheadings, the missing call to action and the missing date/time/place are exactly the leaflet conventions the grid rewards. Coherence around 5 – one undivided block. Range and Accuracy around 4 each: simple sentences plus the frequent, fixable German-into-English errors above. At about 170 words it is also under the ~250 the task expects, which costs another Task Achievement band on its own. The encouraging part: every marker here is trainable, and adding clear subheadings and a real call to action would raise the score quickly.

## Check yourself: quiz

**1. What makes a leaflet look like a leaflet at first glance?**

- One long, unbroken block of text
- **A title plus short sections with subheadings** ✓
- A To / From / Date / Subject header
- A username and a date/time

_Why:_ A leaflet is broken into short, scannable sections under clear subheadings. The layout itself signals the text type – an unbroken block of text reads like an essay instead.

**2. What are the two main purposes of a leaflet?**

- To argue for one side and refute the other
- **To inform AND persuade a specific audience** ✓
- To present survey data objectively
- To confess a personal experience

_Why:_ A leaflet informs the reader about something (an event, a service, a scheme) and persuades them to act on it. Weighing both sides belongs to other text types; neutral data is a report; personal confession is a blog.

**3. Your leaflet is for a youth club aimed at teenagers. What register should you use?**

- Very formal, like a business e-mail
- Neutral and impersonal, like a report
- **Accessible and direct, speaking straight to the reader** ✓
- Academic, with phrases like "It is widely believed that…"

_Why:_ Match register to audience. A youth club leaflet should be warm, direct and full of "you", while staying clean and readable – not slang. Formal or academic phrasing would feel wrong for that reader.

**4. Which sentence works best as the ending of a leaflet?**

- "Taking these points into consideration, the event has clear benefits."
- **"Come along on Saturday – bring a friend and see for yourself."** ✓
- "It is recommended that interested parties consider attendance."
- "In conclusion, this leaflet has described the event."

_Why:_ A leaflet ends with a clear call to action that tells the reader exactly what to do next. The other options are report-style or over-formal closings and give the reader nothing to act on.

**5. A leaflet for an event is nearly finished. What must not be missing?**

- A personal anecdote about the writer
- **The practical details – when, where and how to take part** ✓
- A balanced discussion of pros and cons
- A formal sign-off like "Yours faithfully"

_Why:_ A leaflet has to let the reader act, so the date, time, place, price and contact matter as much as the call to action. Personal stories belong in a blog, weighing pros and cons in an essay, and a formal sign-off in a letter or e-mail.

**6. How is a leaflet different from a report on the same event?**

- A leaflet uses headings; a report never does
- **A leaflet persuades and addresses the reader directly; a report stays neutral and objective** ✓
- A leaflet is always longer than a report
- There is no real difference between them

_Why:_ Both can be well organised, but a report presents facts neutrally for someone who requested them, while a leaflet openly persuades a chosen audience and speaks to them directly with "you" and calls to action.

## Self-check checklist

- [ ] Gave the leaflet a clear title and broke the text into short sections with subheadings.
- [ ] Wrote for a specific audience and matched the register (accessible, not slang, not stiff).
- [ ] Sold the benefits and addressed the reader directly with "you" – not a neutral report.
- [ ] Kept sections short and scannable – no long, unbroken blocks of text.
- [ ] Ended with a clear call to action (come, sign up, get in touch).
- [ ] Included the practical details the reader needs to act: date/time and place for an event, or how to sign up / where to buy / a website for a scheme or product.
