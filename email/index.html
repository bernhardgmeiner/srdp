The e-mail

Clear purpose, proper format

LayoutDo’s and don’tsExampleKey tipPDFPractise
Word count
~250 words (225–275)
Register
Usually formal
Subject line
Required
Sign-off
Yours faithfully / sincerely

Layout

To: – [recipient's e-mail]
From: – [your e-mail]
Date: – [date]
Subject: – [clear and descriptive]
Dear Sir or Madam, / Dear Mr/Ms [Name],
Introduction – state why you are writing in the first sentence
Body paragraphs 1–3 – one content point per paragraph
Conclusion – restate purpose, expected response
Yours faithfully, / Yours sincerely, – faithfully = name unknown; sincerely = name known

Do’s and don’ts

Do

  • Include all header elements (To, From, Date, Subject)
  • State your reason for writing in the first sentence
  • Use formal language – no slang
  • Use full forms (do not instead of don't)
  • Leave a blank line between paragraphs
  • Match greeting to closing (Dear Sir → Yours faithfully)
  • Use "softer" phrases when criticising: I would like to point out, it seems to me

Don’t

  • No contractions in formal e-mails
  • No emojis or emoticons
  • No casual greetings (Hey, Hi there)
  • Don't use an unprofessional e-mail address
  • Don't forget a subject line
  • Don't mix up faithfully/sincerely

Weaker

“Hi! Your headphones broke after two days and I want my money back, thanks.”

Stronger

“Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to request a refund for the headphones I bought on 3 May, which stopped working after two days.”

Why it matters: A formal e-mail names its purpose in the first line and keeps a polite, formal register throughout. The casual version would be marked down for register and tone.

Key tip · Faithfully vs. sincerely: "Dear Sir/Madam" (name unknown) → Yours faithfully. "Dear Mr/Ms Brown" (name known) → Yours sincerely. This mistake is easy to spot. If the task is an informal e-mail (to a friend, a host family, an exchange partner), the same rules apply the other way round: "Hi Anna," / "Dear Tom,", contractions are fine, and you close with "Best wishes" or "Take care". Read the task: the recipient decides the register.

Practise this text type

Pick a task, write it, then let the self-check look at your draft. The task bank has 6 e-mail tasks.

You attended a two-week language course in Brighton that did not match what the website promised.

What the website promised
globalenglish.example – Summer Intensive Brighton

"Small groups of maximum 10 students · all teachers fully qualified with at least five years' experience · modern single rooms with private bathroom, five minutes from the school · full social programme included"

Write an e-mail (around 250 words) to the course provider.

  • compare what was promised with what you actually experienced
  • describe the problems with the accommodation
  • state what you want the company to do

You have read an article in an English-language newspaper and strongly disagree with it.

From the article you are responding to
"The Electric Car Illusion" – The Daily Courier, 3 February 2026

"Electric cars are a pointless trend that will change nothing. They are toys for wealthy city dwellers: too expensive for ordinary families, useless without charging stations, and powered by electricity that still comes from fossil fuels. Politicians who subsidise them are wasting taxpayers' money."

Write a letter to the editor (around 250 words) responding to the article.

  • respond to the article's main claims
  • present your view on the environmental benefits
  • discuss the practical challenges of switching

You have found an advertisement on the website of an international environmental NGO.

The advertisement
greenfuture.example – Summer Internship for School Students

"We are looking for committed students (16+) to support our communications team for four weeks in July. Tasks include researching environmental topics, writing short texts for social media and helping at two public events. Good English, reliability and an interest in climate issues are essential. Apply by e-mail to Ms Hana Said, including your relevant experience and why you want to join us."

Write an e-mail of application (around 250 words) to Ms Said.

  • state which internship you are applying for and where you saw it
  • describe your relevant skills and experience
  • explain why you want to work for this organisation
