Text type · One of two tasks

The report

Present findings and recommend action

LayoutDo’s and don’tsExampleKey tipPDFPractise
Word count
~250 or ~400 words
Register
Formal and neutral
Title
Subject line + section headings
Structure
Header + sections

Layout

Date: – [date]
From: – [your name / role]
Subject: – [clear, descriptive title]
Introduction – ~50–80 words – state purpose and data source
Section + heading – ~50–80 words – main point 1, under its own heading
Section + heading – ~50–80 words – main point 2, under its own heading
Conclusion / Recommendations – ~50–80 words – summarise + suggest action

Do’s and don’ts

Do

  • Present and analyse facts; save your own view for the recommendations
  • Be neutral and keep to the point
  • Use the passive voice where appropriate
  • Include specific data when referencing surveys/statistics – you may invent realistic figures
  • End with clear recommendations or suggestions
  • Make the layout clear: headings and short sections
  • Use a range of linking words

Don’t

  • Don't give personal opinions in the main body
  • Don't repeat information across sections
  • Don't write anything that isn't relevant
  • Don't forget the Date / From / Subject header
  • Don't forget section headings
  • Don't use emotional or informal language

Weaker

“I really think the school canteen is terrible and nobody likes it.”

Stronger

“Key findings: 68 per cent of the 120 students surveyed rated the canteen food as poor or very poor.”

Why it matters: A report stays factual and presents its evidence under meaningful headings. Personal outbursts cost you marks because the register is wrong.

Key tip · You can recognise a report at a glance because of its headings. Give every section a meaningful heading (for example "Key findings"). You do not need to number them.

Practise this text type

Pick a task, write it, then let the self-check look at your draft. The task bank has 4 report tasks.

The student council surveyed students about the school canteen. The principal has asked you to write up the results.

Survey results – 120 students, years 5–8
  • 58% buy lunch in the canteen at least three times a week
  • Food quality rated "poor" or "very poor" by 44%
  • 71% say healthy options sell out before the lunch break
  • Average waiting time: 14 minutes (the lunch break is 25)
  • 63% would pay up to €1 more for better quality

Write a report (around 250 words) for the principal based on these results.

  • present the main findings of the survey
  • analyse the impact on student health and concentration
  • recommend improvements

Your school wants to review how students use technology and has collected questionnaire data.

Questionnaire results – 210 students, years 4–8
  • 92% use a laptop or tablet for schoolwork every day
  • 47% say lessons rarely make use of digital materials
  • Most common uses: research (78%), vocabulary apps (54%), AI tools (49%)
  • 38% admit devices often distract them in class
  • Only 25% have ever been trained in checking online sources

Write a report (around 400 words) for the school management team based on these results.

  • present current usage patterns
  • analyse benefits and problems the data shows
  • recommend guidelines for future use

Your town council asked young residents about local sports facilities. You volunteered to summarise the answers.

Survey results – 85 residents aged 14–19
  • 66% use local sports facilities at least once a week
  • The skate park was rated "in poor condition" by 7 out of 10 users
  • Only 2 of the 6 outdoor pitches have working floodlights
  • 81% would use the sports hall more if there were evening slots

Write a report (around 250 words) for the town council based on these results.

  • present what young people think of the existing facilities
  • identify the main problems the data shows
  • suggest two realistic improvements
