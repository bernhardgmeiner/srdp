The Writing section

You have 120 minutes for two tasks: one longer (~400 words, at B2 usually the essay) and one shorter (~250 words). Writing is one of four equally weighted sections of the written exam, alongside Reading, Listening and Language in Use.

Time split that works: ~65 min for the 400-word task (incl. 5–10 min planning) · ~40 min for the 250-word task · the rest for proofreading both. Write your finishing times on the task sheet before you start.
Text typeWord countRegisterTitle?Key feature
Essay~400 words (360–440)FormalRequiredArgue your position with evidence
Article~250 or ~400 wordsDepends on the audienceRequired – catchy!Write for real readers
Report~250 or ~400 wordsFormal, objective, neutralSubject line + section headingsPresent findings and recommend action
Blog~250 or ~400 wordsInformal to semi-formalPost only (not a comment)Personal voice, invite interaction
E-Mail~250 words (225–275)FormalRequiredClear purpose, proper format
Word count tolerance: ±10%. That is 360–440 words for 400-word tasks and 225–275 for 250-word tasks. If you are further off than that, Task Achievement is reduced by one band.
Almost every task gives three content points. Address each one – in its own body paragraph, and follow whatever your task sheet actually lists.
Register = how formal or casual your language is – the difference between a letter to a company and a message to a friend.
Want the real thing? The tasks on this site are Matura-style, written for practice. The officially released past exam papers live in the BMB download area at matura.gv.at/downloads – same format, real exams.

How you are graded – the four criteria

Each text is rated with the official analytic scale: four independent criteria, each scored 0–10 and equally weighted – weaker areas can be compensated by stronger ones.

01
Task Achievement
Erfüllung der Aufgabenstellung
All content points covered with supporting detail; text type conventions followed; appropriate title/greeting/closing; word count within ±10%.
02
Coherence and Cohesion
Kohärenz und Kohäsion
Clear overall structure; effective paragraphing; logical development; variety of linking devices; clear relationships between sentences.
03
Lexical and Structural Range
Spektrum sprachlicher Mittel
Variety of vocabulary and structures; complex sentence forms; not repeating the same phrases; appropriate register; not lifting from the prompt.
04
Lexical and Structural Accuracy
Sprachrichtigkeit
Grammar control; correct word choice; spelling; punctuation; accurate use of linking devices.
VETO rule: if a text does not address the set task at all (an off-topic or clearly pre-prepared text that ignores the actual prompt), Task Achievement is rated 0 and the other three criteria are not assessed. Writing in the wrong text type alone does not trigger this. Read every task twice.
Don’t play it safe with grammar. A text built only on simple sentences cannot reach the upper Range bands, even if every sentence is correct. Work in a passive, a conditional or a relative clause where it fits naturally.

What the bands mean (simplified)

Everything the task asks for, fully developed with well-chosen details. Conventions confidently used, wide range of vocabulary and complex structures, only occasional slips.

All content points developed with relevant details. Clear structure, good range and variety, errors are rare and never cause misunderstanding.

This is the pass line of the official grid: all points developed but with limited supporting detail, a limited but appropriate set of linking devices, enough range including some complex forms, errors without damage to communication.

Content points addressed but not developed, mostly simple linear sentences, repetition and lifting from the prompt, or errors pile up beyond simple structures.

Only parts of the task recognisable, very limited language, errors make parts of the text hard to understand.

No assessable performance – or the text fails to address the set task (see the VETO rule above).

Simplified from the official B2 rating scale (BMB, 2023 revision). The grid describes levels 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10; the odd levels in between are for texts that sit between two descriptions. Your teachers grade with the full official scale.

The 8 most expensive mistakes

The good news: every one of these is a cheap fix once you can name it.

MistakeWhat it costsThe fix
Writing only 2 of 3 bullet pointsTask Achievement drops sharply→ Tick off each bullet as you write it
Ignoring the text type’s rules (e.g. a report without headings)Task Achievement drops – conventions are part of the criterion→ Check the Layout box in the guide before you start
Copying words from the promptRange drops→ Rephrase the task’s words – use synonyms
Word count off by more than ±10%Task Achievement drops one band→ Count your words. Practise estimating.
Formal style in a blog (or vice versa)Wrong register = lower Task Achievement→ Ask: formal or informal? Then commit.
Only simple linking wordsLow Coherence score→ Use the B2 linking words table
No topic sentencesParagraphs feel random, weak Coherence→ First sentence of each paragraph = the point
Forgetting to proofreadErrors you would catch in 3 minutes→ Always save 5–10 min at the end
