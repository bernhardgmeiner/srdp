Practise

Task bank

24 Matura-style tasks with the material you need: survey figures, short source texts and statements to respond to. Open a task, write it, then check it in the self-check.

The tasks on this site are written for practice in the Matura style. The official past papers are in the BMB download area: matura.gv.at/downloads. Want the full exam under time? Try the mock exam.

Many tasks give you material: survey figures, a quote, an advert or a short text. Use it in your own words. Copying sentences word for word is called lifting. Lifted phrases do not count for Range, and figures you only repeat without a point do not help Task Achievement. Three steps:

  1. Pick the two or three facts that help your content points. You do not have to use everything.
  2. Rephrase them in your own words and grammar (“21% visit monthly” → “Only one student in five uses the library more than once a month”).
  3. Interpret: say what the fact means for your argument or recommendation.
✗ LIFTED
78% of respondents said they valued having a library, only 21% visited it more than once a month. The main reasons given were limited opening hours and a lack of quiet study space.
✓ USED
Almost four in five students say they value the library, yet only one in five actually uses it more than once a month. The gap has two clear causes, and both can be fixed: the doors close too early, and there is nowhere quiet to work.

Quotes are the exception: you may quote a short phrase from a source text if you mark it as a quotation and react to it. The self-check finds sentences copied from the task if you paste the task in as well.

Your class has discussed how tourism is changing European cities.

The statement you are discussing
From a European travel magazine

"Tourism is eating its own children: the more successful a destination becomes, the less liveable it is for the people who call it home."

Write an essay (around 400 words) discussing this statement. Give your essay a title.

  • describe the economic benefits tourism brings to local communities
  • analyse the damage overtourism causes
  • suggest possible solutions and regulations

A youth organisation has invited students to submit essays for its annual publication.

The statement you are discussing
From a youth magazine

"Social media has given teenagers a thousand new friends and taken away their peace of mind."

Write an essay (around 400 words) discussing this statement. Give your essay a title.

  • describe positive aspects of social media use
  • analyse negative effects on self-image and wellbeing
  • evaluate the responsibilities of platforms and parents

Your school is considering introducing a uniform and has asked students for written opinions.

The statement you are discussing
From a student newspaper

"School uniforms do not create equality; they only hide inequality until the school bell rings."

Write an essay (around 400 words) arguing for or against mandatory school uniforms. Give your essay a title.

  • present arguments in favour of uniforms (equality, focus)
  • present arguments against them (expression, cost)
  • state and justify your own position

An international student magazine is collecting essays on democracy and young people.

The statement you are discussing
Heard at a student debate

"If you are old enough to pay taxes on your apprentice wage, you are old enough to vote on what happens with them."

Write an essay (around 400 words) discussing whether 16-year-olds should be allowed to vote in national elections. Give your essay a title.

  • explain why young people's voices matter in politics
  • discuss concerns about maturity and experience
  • evaluate Austria's experience with voting at 16

A parents' association has asked for student perspectives on working while at school.

The statement you are discussing
Overheard at a parents' evening

"A Saturday job teaches a teenager more about real life than a whole year of school."

Write an essay (around 400 words) discussing whether teenagers should have part-time jobs. Give your essay a title.

  • describe the benefits of work experience

The student council surveyed students about the school canteen. The principal has asked you to write up the results.

Survey results – 120 students, years 5–8
  • 58% buy lunch in the canteen at least three times a week
  • Food quality rated "poor" or "very poor" by 44%
  • 71% say healthy options sell out before the lunch break
  • Average waiting time: 14 minutes (the lunch break is 25)
  • 63% would pay up to €1 more for better quality

Write a report (around 250 words) for the principal based on these results.

  • present the main findings of the survey
  • analyse the impact on student health and concentration
  • recommend improvements

Your school wants to review how students use technology and has collected questionnaire data.

Questionnaire results – 210 students, years 4–8
  • 92% use a laptop or tablet for schoolwork every day
  • 47% say lessons rarely make use of digital materials
  • Most common uses: research (78%), vocabulary apps (54%), AI tools (49%)
  • 38% admit devices often distract them in class
  • Only 25% have ever been trained in checking online sources

Write a report (around 400 words) for the school management team based on these results.

  • present current usage patterns
  • analyse benefits and problems the data shows
  • recommend guidelines for future use

Your town council asked young residents about local sports facilities. You volunteered to summarise the answers.

Survey results – 85 residents aged 14–19
  • 66% use local sports facilities at least once a week
  • The skate park was rated "in poor condition" by 7 out of 10 users
  • Only 2 of the 6 outdoor pitches have working floodlights
  • 81% would use the sports hall more if there were evening slots

Write a report (around 250 words) for the town council based on these results.

  • present what young people think of the existing facilities
  • identify the main problems the data shows
  • suggest two realistic improvements

After this year's project week, students completed feedback forms. Your teacher asked you to write up the results.

Feedback results – 52 students, project week
  • Overall rating: 3.1 out of 5
  • Best rated: outdoor activities (4.4 / 5)
  • Worst rated: accommodation (1.9 / 5) and food (2.2 / 5)
  • 60% found the daily programme "too full"
  • 88% would join another project week

Write a report (around 250 words) for your form teacher based on these results.

  • summarise how students rated the project week
  • present the most common criticisms
  • recommend changes for next year

You follow a popular education blog and want to react to its latest post.

The blog post you are commenting on
jamie_teaches · "School should start at 10 a.m. – change my mind" · posted 2 days ago

"Teenagers' body clocks simply aren't made for 8 a.m. lessons. Research keeps telling us that adolescents need more sleep and perform better later in the day, yet schools keep dragging students out of bed at dawn. Start at 10, finish at 5, and everyone wins: better grades, better moods, fewer zombies in first period. Am I wrong?"

Write a comment (around 250 words) responding to Jamie's post. Do not give your comment a title.

  • react to the original post
  • share your own experience with early school starts
  • state what you think schools should do

A food blogger you follow has published a post that is getting a lot of reactions.

The blog post you are commenting on
lena.eats.well · "Keep burgers away from schools!" · posted yesterday

"I'm serious: fast food restaurants should be banned within 500 metres of every school. Walk past any school gate at lunchtime and you'll see queues of teenagers buying burgers, fries and energy drinks. We teach nutrition in biology and then let the burger chains undo it all at lunch. Distance rules work for casinos, so why not for junk food?"

Write a comment (around 250 words) responding to Lena's post. Do not give your comment a title.

  • respond directly to the blogger's argument
  • describe what you observe at your own school
  • suggest a better (or stricter) solution

You attended a two-week language course in Brighton that did not match what the website promised.

What the website promised

"Small groups of maximum 10 students · all teachers fully qualified with at least five years' experience · modern single rooms with private bathroom, five minutes from the school · full social programme included"
