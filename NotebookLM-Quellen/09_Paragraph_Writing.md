# Paragraph writing: the engine of every text

_This source is part of the "Don't Panic, It's Just the Matura" B2 Writing Guide (matura.bernhardgmeiner.com). It follows the official Austrian SRDP documents for the written English Matura (AHS and BHS, level B2)._

Most marks are won or lost inside body paragraphs. This is the four-layer method the whole guide is built on, with worked examples and practice.

## The four layers of a strong body paragraph

**① TOPIC SENTENCE** — States the point of this paragraph in one clear sentence. Everything else must connect back to it.

**② SUPPORTING SENTENCE** — Explains WHY or HOW your point is true. Build the logic: what is the mechanism?

**③ EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE** — Proves it with a concrete fact, statistic, or scenario. Names, numbers, places.

**④ CLOSING SENTENCE** — Draws a consequence or links to the next paragraph. Never just repeat the topic sentence.

## The recipe in four steps

1. **Start with a SPECIFIC claim** — Not a vague observation. Name the exact problem, cause, or consequence you are arguing.
2. **Explain the WHY or HOW** — Build the logic step by step. Your reader should be able to follow the chain of reasoning.
3. **Use CONCRETE evidence** — Names, numbers, places. A real study or local example is always stronger than "many experts say…"
4. **Close with a CONSEQUENCE or NEW INSIGHT** — Never repeat yourself. Look ahead: what happens next? What does this prove? Zoom out.

## Topic-sentence formulas

**CLAIM:** [Topic] + [strong verb/adjective] + [your position]

_Example:_ "The most significant problem with social media is its effect on young people's self-esteem."

**CONTRAST:** While many believe that [X], the evidence suggests [Y].

_Example:_ "While many believe that homework improves performance, the evidence suggests otherwise."

**CAUSE:** One of the main reasons for [X] is [Y].

_Example:_ "One of the main reasons for youth unemployment is the mismatch between education and the job market."

## Weak topic sentences to avoid

- "There are many problems." → Too vague – which problems? Name them.
- "Technology has changed the world." → Too broad – narrow it to your specific argument.
- "Is social media bad?" → A question, not a claim. Make a statement.

## Three ways to support your point

**EXPLANATION** (Answer "why?"): "This is largely because teenagers compare themselves to carefully edited images, which creates unrealistic expectations."

**EVIDENCE** (Use data or facts): "A 2017 report by the Royal Society for Public Health ranked Instagram the worst platform for the mental health of 14- to 24-year-olds."

**ILLUSTRATION** (Paint a scenario): "Consider a typical teenager who spends two hours a day scrolling – by the end of the week, they will have been exposed to hundreds of idealised images."

## A paragraph, layer by layer

- **point:** Social media can be very harmful to teenagers' mental health.
- **explain:** Apps like Instagram and TikTok are full of edited photos and perfect-looking lives. When young people see this every day, they start to believe that their own lives are not good enough, which makes them feel unhappy and insecure.
- **evidence:** A 2017 report by the Royal Society for Public Health ranked Instagram as the most harmful social media platform for young people's mental health and body image.
- **closing:** If platforms do not do more to protect young users, these problems will only get worse in the future.

## Closing sentences

**Strong closings:**

- Draw a consequence: "Schools must act before the problem gets worse."
- Link forward: "The economic impact is only part of the picture."
- Reinforce the point: "The cost of fast fashion can no longer be ignored."

**Weak closings:**

- "So, as I said, social media is bad for self-esteem." → Just repeats the topic sentence
- "Also, the government should invest more in education." → Introduces a new idea – that's the next paragraph
- "…and that is why this is an important topic." → Vague filler – says nothing

## Strong vs. weak paragraph (same topic)

**Strong:**

> Individual lifestyle choices can make a difference because they change what companies produce and sell. When many people decide to eat less meat, businesses notice and start offering more sustainable options. In Austria, demand for plant-based food has grown so much that supermarkets like BILLA and SPAR now have large vegan sections that did not exist five years ago. Even small choices can add up to big changes when enough people make them at the same time.

**Weak:**

> Individual choices are important. Many people say that what we do in daily life matters. Some experts think that eating less meat is good. So people should make better choices because this is important for the environment and for our future.

**What makes the difference:**

- The weak paragraph has no concrete topic sentence – "individual choices are important" says nothing specific.
- It uses vague attribution ("many people say", "some experts think") instead of actual evidence.
- "This is important for our future" is a non-conclusion – it repeats a vague claim instead of drawing a real consequence.
- The strong paragraph names real places (BILLA, SPAR), explains a mechanism (people buy → companies adapt), and ends with a new insight.

## Phrases for building paragraphs

**Starting a paragraph:** "To begin with,…"; "First and foremost,…"; "One of the main reasons for this is…"; "A key factor to consider is…"; "It is widely acknowledged that…"; "While many argue that…, the evidence suggests…"

**Explaining / developing:** "This is largely because…"; "The main reason for this is that…"; "This can be explained by the fact that…"; "In other words,…"; "What this means in practice is that…"; "This suggests that…"

**Introducing evidence:** "According to…"; "Research shows that…"; "Studies have found that…"; "It has been reported that…"; "A recent survey by… found that…"; "For example,…"; "For instance,…"

**Closing a paragraph:** "This clearly demonstrates that…"; "It is therefore evident that…"; "This leads to the conclusion that…"; "Given this evidence, it is difficult to argue that…"; "This highlights the need for…"; "Unless something changes,…"

## Warm-ups (build the missing topic sentence)

Each warm-up gives you a supporting sentence, evidence and a closing. Your job is the topic sentence; several model versions follow.

### Warm-up 1: School Uniforms

- Supporting: Many students say that their clothes are a way to show who they are, and wearing the same outfit every day would take that away from them.
- Evidence: In countries like the UK and Japan, where uniforms are common, students often report feeling pressure to express their individuality in other ways, such as through hairstyles or accessories.
- Closing: For teenagers, the freedom to choose what to wear is about more than fashion – it is about developing a sense of identity.

_Model topic sentences:_

- (CLAIM) One of the strongest arguments against school uniforms is that they limit students' ability to express themselves.
- (CONTRAST) While uniforms may create a sense of equality, they also take away an important form of self-expression for young people.
- (CLAIM) The biggest problem with school uniforms is that they ignore the role clothing plays in teenage identity.
- (CONTRAST) Although supporters of uniforms focus on discipline, they often overlook how important personal style is for teenagers.
- (CAUSE) Forcing students to wear the same clothes every day sends the message that individuality does not matter.

### Warm-up 2: Smartphones in School

- Supporting: When a phone buzzes during a lesson, most students will look at it immediately, even if they know they should not. This interrupts not only their own learning but also the concentration of everyone around them.
- Evidence: A study from the University of Texas found that students who had their phones on their desk – even turned off – performed worse on tests than those whose phones were in another room.
- Closing: Unless schools find a way to deal with this distraction, students will continue to lose valuable learning time every single day.

_Model topic sentences:_

- (CLAIM) Smartphones are one of the biggest distractions in today's classrooms.
- (CLAIM) Even when students try to focus, the presence of a smartphone makes it much harder to concentrate on the lesson.
- (CONTRAST) While phones can be useful learning tools, their negative effect on concentration in the classroom is hard to deny.
- (CAUSE) The main reason why many teachers want to ban phones is that they constantly interrupt the learning process.
- (CONTRAST) Although most students believe they can multitask, research shows that phones seriously reduce their ability to focus.

### Warm-up 3: Fast Food & Health

- Supporting: A typical fast food meal contains more calories, fat, and sugar than what a teenager needs for an entire day. Eating this regularly makes it very difficult to maintain a healthy weight.
- Evidence: In Austria, the number of overweight young people has increased steadily over the past decade, and health experts say that the growing popularity of fast food chains is one of the key reasons.
- Closing: If young people do not learn to make healthier food choices now, they risk developing serious health problems later in life.

_Model topic sentences:_

- (CAUSE) The growing popularity of fast food is one of the main reasons why more and more young people have health problems.
- (CONTRAST) Fast food may be cheap and convenient, but it comes at a high cost to young people's health.
- (CLAIM) One of the most serious consequences of the fast food trend is its effect on teenagers' physical health.
- (CONTRAST) While fast food is often seen as a harmless treat, eating it regularly can lead to serious health issues for young people.
- (CAUSE) The reason so many teenagers struggle with their weight is directly connected to how often they eat fast food.

## Full practice paragraphs (with examiner-style analysis)

### Task 1: Social Media & Mental Health

**Prompt:** "Social media has changed the way young people communicate. Discuss the impact of social media on teenagers' mental health."

**Task:** Write ONE complete body paragraph addressing this topic. Use all four layers.

**Model paragraph:**

> Social media can be very harmful to teenagers' mental health. Apps like Instagram and TikTok are full of edited photos and perfect-looking lives. When young people see this every day, they start to believe that their own lives are not good enough, which makes them feel unhappy and insecure. A 2017 report by the Royal Society for Public Health ranked Instagram as the most harmful social media platform for young people's mental health and body image. If platforms do not do more to protect young users, these problems will only get worse in the future.

_Why it works:_

- **Topic sentence:** Clear and specific: not "Social media is bad" but narrows it down to mental health.
- **Supporting sentence:** Explains the HOW: edited photos → unrealistic expectations → feeling insecure. The reader follows the logic.
- **Evidence:** Names a real, citable study instead of a vague "many experts say...". Only quote a number if you can stand behind it.
- **Closing:** Doesn't repeat the topic sentence. Instead, it looks ahead: what happens if nothing changes? That's a real conclusion.

### Task 2: Politicians on Social Media

**Prompt:** "More and more politicians use social media to share their opinions directly with the public. Discuss whether this is a positive or negative development."

**Task:** Write ONE complete body paragraph addressing this topic. Use all four layers.

**Model paragraph:**

> One serious problem with politicians using social media is that they can spread false information without anyone checking it first. On platforms like X or Instagram, there are no journalists or editors who verify what is being posted. This means that politicians can make claims that sound convincing but are not actually true, and millions of people will see them instantly. For example, during elections in the US and Europe, many political posts were later found to contain false or misleading statements, but by then they had already been shared thousands of times. This makes it harder for voters to tell the difference between facts and opinions, which is a real danger for democracy.

_Why it works:_

- **Topic sentence:** Uses the CLAIM formula: identifies one specific problem (false information). Keeps it simple and focused.
- **Supporting sentence:** Explains the mechanism in plain language: no fact-checking → false claims → millions see them. Easy to follow.
- **Evidence:** Uses a general but concrete example (elections in US and Europe). You don't need to name one person – the pattern is enough.
- **Closing:** Adds a real consequence: voters can't tell facts from opinions. The paragraph ends on a bigger idea, not a repetition.

### Task 3: Climate & Lifestyle

**Prompt:** "Many people argue that individual lifestyle choices can help fight climate change. Discuss whether personal actions like eating less meat or avoiding flights can make a real difference."

**Task:** Write ONE complete body paragraph addressing this topic. Use all four layers.

**Model paragraph:**

> Individual lifestyle choices can make a difference because they change what companies produce and sell. When many people decide to eat less meat or to take the train instead of a flight, businesses notice and start to offer more climate-friendly options. This creates a cycle: the more people buy sustainable products, the cheaper and more available they become. In Austria, for example, the demand for plant-based food has grown so much that supermarkets like BILLA and SPAR now have large vegan sections that did not exist five years ago. Even small choices can add up to big changes when enough people make them at the same time.

_Why it works:_

- **Topic sentence:** Uses a CAUSE formula: personal choices matter BECAUSE they change what companies do. A clear argument in one sentence.
- **Supporting sentence:** Explains the logic step by step: people buy differently → companies adapt → products become cheaper. The chain makes sense.
- **Evidence:** Uses a local, relatable example (BILLA, SPAR). Your examiners will notice you are not recycling generic facts.
- **Closing:** Short and punchy. Doesn't repeat the topic sentence but zooms out: small + many = big. A satisfying ending.
